## DPZ - Domino's Pizza

Discovery lane: reference | Market cap bucket: large_cap | Attention: high_attention

**中文 Thesis:** 事实支持的推论：Domino's 不应只被视为披萨品牌或轻资产特许经营公司。其美国和加拿大供应链中心向加盟店销售食品、面团和其他产品，2025 年供应链收入约 29.90 亿美元，占合并收入约 60.5%；供应链毛利约 3.447 亿美元，毛利率约 11.5%。如果该体系持续帮助加盟商降低采购复杂度、稳定门店产品一致性，并在食材波动和促销环境下保护单店经济模型，那么“commissary / 供应链中心”就是餐饮加盟体系中的真实经营卡点。该结论是参考样本结论，不构成对 DPZ 的交易建议。

**English Thesis:** Fact-supported inference: Domino's should not be analyzed only as a pizza brand or asset-light franchisor. Its U.S. and Canadian supply-chain centers sell food, dough and other products to franchised stores. In 2025, supply-chain revenue was approximately $2.99 billion, or about 60.5% of consolidated revenue; supply-chain gross margin was about $344.7 million, or 11.5%. If this system keeps reducing franchisee procurement complexity, stabilizing product consistency and protecting store-level economics through commodity and promotional cycles, then commissary/supply-chain control is a real restaurant operating chokepoint. This is a reference-case thesis, not a trading recommendation.

Supply-chain position: Restaurant franchise system infrastructure layer: brand demand generation plus franchised store network plus vertically integrated procurement, dough/food preparation, supply-chain centers, distribution, digital ordering, loyalty and store operating standards. DPZ sits downstream as a consumer restaurant brand but also midstream as a captive foodservice supplier to its franchisees.

Why underfollowed: Not underfollowed at the company level. The under-discussed angle is that DPZ is partly a foodservice supply-chain and commissary operator rather than only a royalty stream or pizza concept. This reference case can help identify lower-attention restaurant companies, distributors, commissary operators, bakery suppliers, cold-chain providers, foodservice technology vendors or franchisors whose control point is hidden one layer behind the consumer-facing brand.

Hidden signal to verify: Supply-chain gross margin stability, franchisee food-cost advantage, franchisee reliance on Domino's supply-chain centers, direct digital ordering economics, loyalty participation, carryout versus delivery mix, franchisee store-level profitability, net unit openings and closure rates.

Risks:

- Food-cost volatility, especially cheese and other core ingredients, could pressure supply-chain gross margin or franchisee margins.
- Promotion-driven same-store sales could mask weaker operator returns if discounts reduce franchisee profitability.
- Delivery mix and third-party aggregator usage could weaken first-party ordering economics and reduce the benefit of Domino's digital infrastructure.
- Insurance, labor, transportation or fuel costs could offset procurement gains inside the supply-chain segment.
- Franchisee dissatisfaction with required purchasing, pricing or service levels could weaken the captive-supply-chain thesis.
- International markets may not replicate the U.S./Canada supply-chain model because many are operated through master franchise structures with different procurement systems.
- A high-attention large-cap reference case may already be well understood by the market; the research value is primarily in mapping the model to less-followed analogs.

Falsification plan:

- Downgrade the reference thesis if supply-chain gross margin percentage declines for multiple periods despite revenue growth, implying poor cost pass-through or weaker procurement leverage.
- Downgrade if U.S. franchisee profitability deteriorates while net unit growth slows, closures rise or franchisee commentary turns negative.
- Downgrade if same-store sales are driven mainly by heavy promotions without evidence of healthy order counts, traffic or franchisee returns.
- Downgrade if delivery economics worsen because third-party aggregator mix or delivery labor costs rise faster than direct digital and carryout economics can offset.
- Downgrade if franchisees seek alternative sourcing, challenge supply-chain pricing, or disclose dissatisfaction with required purchases.
- Downgrade if food inflation, transportation costs or insurance costs structurally compress supply-chain margin and reduce the value of scale procurement.
- Upgrade the broader restaurant-chokepoint thesis if DPZ maintains supply-chain margin, positive unit growth and healthy franchisee economics through a commodity-cost cycle.
