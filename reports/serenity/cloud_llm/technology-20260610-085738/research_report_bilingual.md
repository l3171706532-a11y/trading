**English Thesis:** [Inference, now partly supported by facts] HBM economics raise the value of known-good-die screening before expensive stacking. As stack height, yield sensitivity, and package cost rise, advanced wafer-level test and customized probe cards can become a hidden manufacturing bottleneck. FormFactor does not sell HBM; it sells test capability that may be required to manufacture HBM economically. Q1 2026 evidence supports the thesis: total revenue rose 32.0% year over year, DRAM probe-card revenue rose 69.7%, and the company’s 10-Q attributed DRAM growth primarily to increased demand for HBM designs used in generative AI. The thesis is not fully proven because investors still need to separate durable HBM test-intensity growth from a normal DRAM cycle, one-customer pull-forward, or temporary capacity tightness.

Supply-chain position: AI accelerators and HPC systems -> HBM producers such as SK hynix, Samsung, Micron -> wafer fabrication and HBM die production -> wafer-level electrical test and known-good-die screening -> FormFactor probe cards, MEMS contact elements, probe stations, thermal systems and related test support -> advanced packaging and HBM stacking. FormFactor sits one layer below visible HBM producers and one or two steps away from the obvious AI GPU demand driver.

Why underfollowed: FormFactor’s AI exposure is hidden behind semiconductor test-equipment labels, probe-card terminology, wafer-level contact mechanics, MEMS probes, and customer-specific design cycles. The market usually sees HBM producers, GPUs, advanced packaging, or memory pricing first. FormFactor is less obvious because its revenue signal is embedded inside DRAM probe-card growth, foundry/logic probe-card growth, geographic shipment mix, customer concentration, and gross-margin mix. It also has non-HBM businesses such as legacy systems, flash, cryogenic systems, probe stations, and foundry/logic products, which blur the purity of the HBM thesis.

Hidden signal to verify: The key hidden signal is whether HBM-related DRAM probe-card demand is growing faster than non-HBM DRAM, NAND/Flash, foundry/logic and systems revenue, and whether that growth is supported by multiple leading memory customers rather than mostly by SK hynix. In Q1 2026, DRAM probe-card revenue grew 69.7% year over year versus total revenue growth of 32.0%, while systems revenue fell 19.9%; this is a positive but not yet sufficient signal because FormFactor does not disclose HBM-only revenue.

Risks:

- SK hynix has been a major customer, and Q1 2026 South Korea revenue strength points to Korean DRAM/HBM demand. If one customer pulls forward probe-card orders and later normalizes, the apparent HBM growth could reverse quickly.
- FormFactor does not disclose HBM-only revenue. DRAM growth can include non-HBM DDR4, DDR5 or other DRAM designs, and the company’s Q4 2025 release explicitly noted non-HBM DRAM strength.
- Probe cards are tied to semiconductor design ramps, wafer starts, unit volumes and test strategies. A memory downturn, HBM capacity digestion phase, or lower-than-expected AI accelerator demand could reduce orders.
- The 10-K states that changes in customer test strategies, equipment, design-for-testability, self-test or later-stage testing can reduce probe-card demand.
- Other probe-card suppliers may win HBM platforms or pressure pricing. The thesis requires evidence that FormFactor retains technical share in next-generation HBM designs.
- Q1 2026 probe-card gross margin improved strongly, but GAAP gross margin was affected by restructuring charges and tariffs. If favorable product mix does not persist, the HBM mix thesis weakens.
- The company is consolidating sites and bringing the Farmers Branch, Texas facility online late in fiscal 2026. Start-up costs, yield issues, delays or labor constraints could offset demand strength.
- The company relies on limited or sole-source suppliers for key materials and components such as substrates, printed circuit boards, contact elements and interconnects.
- At a roughly $9.6 billion market capitalization and high reported P/E as of June 10, 2026, the market may already be pricing in part of the HBM and AI test story. This report is research support, not a buy/sell recommendation.

Falsification plan:

- Falsify or downgrade if Q2-Q4 2026 DRAM probe-card growth decelerates toward total company growth while management stops emphasizing HBM or advanced memory as the primary driver.
- Falsify or downgrade if non-HBM DRAM, DDR4/DDR5 recovery, Flash or generic memory-cycle demand explains most incremental revenue rather than HBM-related designs.
- Falsify or downgrade if customer concentration remains dominated by one HBM customer and no evidence emerges of design wins or revenue contribution from multiple leading memory manufacturers.
- Falsify or downgrade if Probe Cards gross margin fails to remain structurally above prior-cycle levels despite claimed favorable HBM mix, after adjusting for restructuring, tariffs and utilization.
- Falsify or downgrade if competitors win key HBM4/HBM4E probe-card platforms or if customers qualify alternative suppliers that reduce FormFactor’s share.
- Falsify or downgrade if new HBM architectures reduce wafer-level test intensity, rely more on built-in self-test, move more value to downstream package test, or otherwise reduce FormFactor’s role.
- Falsify or downgrade if Farmers Branch ramp or site consolidation causes delivery misses, customer dissatisfaction, yield problems or elevated costs that impair the ability to serve HBM demand.
- Falsify or downgrade if management guidance shows revenue growth led by Foundry & Logic or Systems without continued DRAM/HBM acceleration, because that would change the thesis from HBM bottleneck to broader semiconductor test recovery.
