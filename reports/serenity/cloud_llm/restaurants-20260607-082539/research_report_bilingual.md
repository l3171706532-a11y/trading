## TOST - Toast, Inc.

**English Thesis:** Toast’s chokepoint is not a standalone POS terminal or merchant-acquiring function; it is the integrated restaurant operating layer spanning POS, payments, subscription software, online ordering, marketing, loyalty, labor/payroll, inventory, capital products, AI, and guest engagement. In 2026, restaurants continue to face cost inflation, uneven traffic, cautious consumers, swipe-fee pressure, and labor constraints, making tools that improve throughput, repeat visits, labor productivity, menu control, and operating visibility more mission-critical. Toast reported Q1 2026 ARR growth of 26% year over year to about $2.2B, locations growth of 22% to about 171,000, GPV growth of 22% to $51.3B, and raised full-year guidance for non-GAAP subscription-services and fintech-solutions gross profit and adjusted EBITDA. The inference is that Toast may be a high-purity public-market proxy for the restaurant operating-system bottleneck. The main unresolved question is whether growth is driven by durable module attach and retention, not merely location expansion, restaurant inflation, or larger payment volume.

Supply-chain position: Theme map / 产业链位置: Restaurants → restaurant operators → front-of-house and back-of-house workflows → POS and order capture → payments and settlement → guest data, loyalty, marketing, online ordering, delivery integrations → labor, payroll, scheduling, inventory, menu and food-cost control → analytics/AI and capital products. Toast sits in the software-fintech operating layer between restaurants and their customers, staff, payment networks, banks, delivery channels, and suppliers. This is a horizontal workflow chokepoint because it touches transaction capture, payment monetization, restaurant data, customer retention, and operating decisions. Public-market mapping: Toast is the focused pure-play; adjacent/competitive mappings include Block/Square, Fiserv/Clover, Oracle MICROS, NCR Voyix/Aloha, PAR Technology, Lightspeed, Shift4, Olo, DoorDash/Uber Eats integrations, Restaurant365, and enterprise in-house systems.

Risks:

- Restaurant operator health could deteriorate. If independent restaurants face weaker traffic, higher failure rates, or reduced discretionary software spend, Toast may see slower net adds, higher churn, or lower expansion revenue.
- GPV growth can be flattered by menu-price inflation. If transaction count, same-location sales, or GPV per location weaken, headline GPV growth may overstate platform health.
- The operating-system thesis depends on customers adopting multiple modules beyond POS and payment processing. If payroll, marketing, loyalty, inventory, online ordering, and AI attach rates stall, Toast may remain closer to a payment/POS vendor than a full operating system.
- Fintech revenue and gross profit depend on payment volume, take rate, processing costs, network fees, risk losses, and merchant pricing. Competitive pressure or regulatory/card-fee pressure could compress economics.
- Square, Clover/Fiserv, Oracle MICROS, NCR Voyix/Aloha, PAR, Lightspeed, Shift4, Olo, Restaurant365, and enterprise in-house stacks may target the same restaurant workflows. Generalist payment platforms can compete on price; legacy enterprise systems can compete on installed base and customization.
- Enterprise, drive-thru, retail food-and-beverage, and international expansion may require different product features, sales cycles, implementation support, and integrations than Toast’s core SMB restaurant base.
- Even if the business performs well, public-market expectations may already price in strong ARR growth, margin expansion, AI monetization, and enterprise expansion. This report does not provide a buy/sell view or target price.
- Key indicators such as net revenue retention, churn by cohort, module attach, AI monetization, and segment-level unit economics may not be fully disclosed, making the chokepoint thesis harder to verify from public data alone.

Falsification plan:

- Downgrade the thesis if net location additions slow materially for two or more quarters without offsetting ARPU, attach-rate, or margin improvement.
- Downgrade the thesis if GPV growth remains positive but GPV per location, same-location GPV, or transaction activity deteriorates, suggesting weak restaurant throughput rather than true platform expansion.
- Downgrade the thesis if subscription ARR or subscription gross profit decelerates faster than location growth, because that would weaken the claim that restaurants are attaching more software modules.
- Downgrade the thesis if management commentary or filings show rising churn, weaker net revenue retention, longer implementation cycles, or lower customer satisfaction among SMB restaurants.
- Downgrade the thesis if AI, marketing, payroll, inventory, loyalty, and ordering products fail to show measurable adoption, revenue contribution, or ROI evidence.
- Downgrade the thesis if competitive pricing from Square, Clover, Oracle, NCR Voyix, PAR, Lightspeed, Shift4, or vertical software vendors forces lower take rates, higher incentives, or margin compression.
- Downgrade the thesis if enterprise and international expansion require disproportionate sales, support, or R&D investment and dilute operating leverage.
- Downgrade the thesis if restaurant-industry indicators show broad SMB closures, weakening traffic, or sharp reductions in technology budgets.
